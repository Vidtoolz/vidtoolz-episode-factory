# MiniMax musical continuity doctrine

A generated cue is one continuous composition. Its arrangement may evolve substantially, but it must remain recognizably the same piece unless the approved music brief explicitly calls for a major transformation.

## Identity anchors

Keep the core instrumental palette, tonal and harmonic world, motif family, groove family, production aesthetic, and spatial character coherent across the cue.

## Allowed evolution

Intensity, density, layers, register, fills, rhythmic and harmonic development, melodic variation, breakdown/rebuild, climax, and ending resolution may change over time.

## Forbidden by default

Do not introduce an unrelated second composition, sudden genre switch, abrupt palette replacement, disconnected groove, unmotivated tonal reset, hard musical restart, or unrelated late theme.

> Evolve the same cue; do not replace it.

The deterministic MiniMax caption adapter states this rule once for the whole arrangement and tells every section after the opening to inherit the established motif, palette, harmony, and groove. This is a generation constraint, not an automated aesthetic verdict; human listening remains authoritative.
